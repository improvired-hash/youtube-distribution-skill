# India playbook

## Language: choose by the viewer you want

| Target viewer | Language | Think of |
|---|---|---|
| Tier-1, corporate, decision-makers | **English** | B2B SaaS, premium brands, investor audiences |
| Mass India, wide reach | **Hinglish** | Consumer apps, marketplaces, D2C at scale |
| Tier-3, regional | **Hindi or the regional language** that's dominant there | Regional D2C, vernacular finance, local services |

Decide by who should watch, not by what the founder speaks most comfortably. A brand can run two channels, or two series, for two tiers. Don't mix languages randomly on one channel.

## Scoring and search

- vidIQ scores English above Hinglish. Compare only within one language (`vidiq-playbook.md`).
- Set the region code for India (`in`) consistently in research tools.
- Hinglish titles: write them the way people type them in the Roman script, unless the audience searches in Devanagari.

## Cultural moments

Festival and regional-identity hooks travel for brands when they're about the viewer, not the offer. Real proof: ixigo's Dussehra and "Kanpuriya friends" Shorts did 70-117x its organic baseline, while its sale-announcement Shorts got about 150-230 views (`examples.md`).

## Commercial calendar

Plan content toward pricing events (sales, low-ticket launches) every 6-8 weeks (`workflows/attribution.md`). In India, also map them against festivals and sale seasons in your category.
