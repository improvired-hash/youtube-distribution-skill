# Attribution and reporting

## 1. Judge by the outcome

Brand building → views from the right audience are fine. Sales → leads, demo bookings and purchases. See `strategy.md`.

## 2. Minimum attribution setup (every founder, no excuses)

Perfect attribution isn't possible. YouTube influence mostly shows up indirectly. Capture it three ways:

1. **Ask:** a "How did you hear about us?" field on every form and at checkout, with **YouTube as an explicit option**.
2. **Listen:** log every sales call where the prospect says "I saw your video". Sales teams hear it constantly and never write it down. Make it a CRM field.
3. **Track:** a UTM or short tracked link (e.g. bit.ly) in every description and pinned comment, unique per video.

Use a **90-day lag window**: people watch, then buy weeks later.

## 3. Commercial events beat content volume

The best lead months usually come from **pricing events** (sales, low-ticket launches), not from more uploads. Run a structured sale cadence, **every 6-8 weeks**, and plan content to build up to each event, instead of raising volume evenly. Real proof: ixigo's "19th Birthday Travel Sale" announcement Shorts got about 150-230 views organically. The sale drives revenue; the announcement video doesn't (`references/examples.md`).

## 4. Dashboards: a windshield, not a rearview mirror

Every report answers "what do we do next week?" Leadership gets **one recommendation to approve or reject**, not a dashboard to explore.

## 5. Weekly report template

```
Week of <date>

<Channel / IP>   WIN | BLOCKED | SWITCHED  — <one line why>
<Channel / IP>   WIN | BLOCKED | SWITCHED  — <one line why>

Shorts: <one headline insight, with numbers>
Leads from YouTube: <form "YouTube" answers> + <sales-call mentions> + <tracked link conversions>
Recommendation: <one decision to approve or reject>
```

## 6. Monthly: dual confidence scores

The operator and the manager each score every area (packaging, Shorts, distribution, leads) from 1-10 **independently**, then compare. The gaps are the agenda.
