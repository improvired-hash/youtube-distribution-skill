# Distribution: clipping, seeding, vendors, ads

**The machine: YouTube virality → clip virality → Instagram seeding virality.** Same story, three times the reach.

## 1. Owned, earned, paid: sequence by stage

| Stage | Priority |
|---|---|
| No proven content yet | **Owned**: find formats that work organically first. Paid can't rescue content that doesn't work. |
| A few proven winners | **Earned**: clip the winners, seed them through niche pages and creators, collaborations |
| Repeatable winners | **Paid**: put money behind content that already works organically |

Most brands start on paid and never build owned. Flag it if the audit shows mostly likely-paid views.

## 2. Clipping and fan-page vendors

Pick on **page-network depth and data honesty**, not price.

### Ask for this before paying
- **Screen recordings** of Instagram Insights or YouTube Studio, not screenshots: reach, accounts reached, % non-followers, audience location, age.
- Per-post data for their last 20 posts, not just the hits.
- 2-3 past campaign reports with the client's own tracked results.

### Red flags (heuristics: one alone isn't proof, two or more means walk away)
| Check | Red flag |
|---|---|
| Like rate on Reels | Under ~1% on posts with big view counts |
| Views vs followers | One post 50x the others while the rest are flat, or views consistently far above what the following can explain |
| Story views | Under ~2-3% of followers usually means bought or dead followers |
| Comments | Generic ("nice", "🔥🔥"), repeated, or from accounts outside the claimed audience (e.g. foreign accounts on Indian content) |
| Audience location | Doesn't match the claimed market |
| Follower growth | Sudden step jumps with no viral post to explain them |

Mega-pages are the most likely to inflate views. Cross-check every claim against the raw data before paying.

### Pilot before any contract
Pay for **one or two posts** first, with a tracked link or UTM and a promo code unique to that vendor. Pay the rest only on verified reach, from screen-recorded insights, plus the clicks and codes you can see on your side. A vendor who refuses a pilot or a screen recording is the answer.

## 3. Control the narrative

Build or partner with a network of niche pages in your category, so the same story reaches people from several trusted sources.

## 4. YouTube ads

- **What they're for:** reach and retargeting, bought like Meta ads (through Google Ads). Use them to scale videos that already win organically, to retarget site visitors and video viewers, and to amplify sale events. Not to manufacture "views" on content that doesn't work.
- **Ad views don't build an organic audience.** Real proof: CRED's top Shorts show 23.9M and 13.9M views with 0.00-0.04% likes, while its organic Shorts baseline is about 2.8K (`references/examples.md`).
- **Isolate paid traffic:**
  1. **Best:** run ads from a separate, smaller channel, so ad viewers don't pollute the main channel's audience graph. Exception: flagship product videos.
  2. **At least:** in YouTube Studio → Analytics → Advanced mode, filter traffic sources to exclude **YouTube advertising** (and playlist traffic where relevant) before reading any performance numbers.
